Source errors and unresolved clinical items

14The recording marks Bankart repair, but a Bankart repair addresses the glenoid labrum and shoulder instability. This is not a sound answer to a question asking for rotator cuff repair. None of the offered choices supplies the appropriate rotator cuff procedure, so this item is not scored.
18The recording identifies fish allergy as a reason for caution with protamine. Its explanation contains a medication-unit error: approximately 1 mg of protamine neutralizes 100 units of unfractionated heparin, not 100 mg. Actual reversal dosing depends on the heparin exposure and elapsed time and requires the applicable product monograph and protocol.
41The recording selects a prion-specific surface-decontamination chemical option. ORNAC directs teams to Health Canada’s prion guidance and coordinated IPAC/MDR procedures; it does not establish the exact concentrations and contact time in this question. This item is review only until that specific Canadian protocol is checked.
52The recorded exposure time is four minutes. ORNAC requires cycle, device, and container parameters to follow validated instructions and requires a closed rigid IUSS container. This generic unwrapped-cycle question does not establish a usable Canadian processing instruction and is review only.
65The recorded generic nonporous IUSS exposure time is three minutes. ORNAC requires validated cycle-specific instructions, a closed rigid container, and an urgent unplanned need. The time alone is not a Canadian processing prescription; review only.
69The recorded generic gravity-cycle exposure is three minutes. ORNAC requires validated device, cycle, and container instructions and restricts IUSS to urgent unplanned needs. Review only because the stated temperature/time does not establish a complete Canadian process.
70The recording gives ten minutes for this generic porous/lumened gravity-cycle question. ORNAC requires validated load-specific parameters and a closed rigid IUSS container. Review only; do not substitute a memorized generic time for the instructions.
87The recording selects therapeutic touch and describes a manipulable human energy field. That proposed mechanism and the claimed clinical benefits are not established by the ORNAC document. This item is kept for source review and is not scored as an evidence-based clinical claim.
94Midazolam and diazepam are benzodiazepines. The recording’s explanation says retrograde amnesia; the midazolam monograph describes anterograde amnesia, meaning impaired memory for events after administration.
96The recording selects sensitivity to soybean oil, egg lecithin, or glycerol. ORNAC does not establish this ingredient-specific contraindication. Ingredient hypersensitivity, food allergy, and a previous reaction to propofol must not be treated as interchangeable; check the actual formulation and anaesthesia assessment. Review only.
107The stem gives incompatible weights: 188 lb is about 85.3 kg, not 75 kg. The recording marks size 4 using its 75-kg table; the pounds value would fall in a different row. Device-specific LMA instructions also vary. No single answer is justified by the inconsistent stem, so this is not scored.
116The recording specifies a minimum of 18 hours daily unless care requires removal. That numeric minimum was not found in ORNAC; ORNAC emphasizes the selected device’s instructions, appropriate fitting, activation before induction, monitoring, and handoff. Review only as a numeric policy claim.
141The recording marks current, but the wording does not distinguish current density, contact area, resistance, and the generator’s response. Its explanation also generalizes a return to ground rather than the intended generator circuit. ORNAC does not resolve these options into a unique answer; this ambiguous item is not scored.
163The recording gives four to six days for delayed primary closure after debridement. ORNAC’s wound-management section does not establish a fixed interval for this scenario. Closure depends on wound condition and the surgical plan; review only rather than teaching a guaranteed return date.
179The recording selects increased temperature, heart rate, and respiratory rate. The explanation’s assumption that atelectasis causes postoperative fever is not supported by the cited clinical research. Fever requires assessment for its cause rather than attribution to atelectasis; this item is not scored.
188The recording marks all of the above and treats amniotic-fluid exposure as an absolute exclusion. Modern obstetric cell-salvage guidance permits use under defined protocols and processing precautions. The options do not capture those distinctions, so the item is not scored; contaminating agents still require the actual device and institutional protocol.
189A ruptured AAA can cause massive haemorrhage, making urgent blood-bank coordination essential. The recording incorrectly equates rupture with aortic dissection; these are distinct conditions, although either can be life-threatening.
197The recording selects a fall greater than ten feet as a high-risk mechanism. ORNAC does not establish this cutoff, and lower falls can still cause serious injury. Review only; assess the full mechanism and patient rather than using this as a safe-versus-unsafe threshold.
199The recording selects chemical protection for deployable facilities. The vague statement does not establish which nuclear, biological, or chemical hazards a particular system protects against. ORNAC does not validate that broad claim; review only.

Evidence and editorial method

Question numbers and choice order are preserved, and the green correct-answer indicator is distinguished from the learner’s selected answer. OCR was corrected using the recording, including faint text and later repeated views. Explanations are edited study summaries, with source errors corrected or flagged; they are not verbatim transcripts. No missing text is invented for the withheld item.

The supplied ORNAC PDF is the comparison authority. Printed-page labels are included because PDF page numbers differ. Specific clinical corrections also use the following primary sources:

  • AAOS OrthoInfo: glenoid labrum tear
  • Health Canada product monograph: heparin sodium, protamine reversal
  • Protamine prescribing information (2026): fish-allergy precaution and dosing
  • PHAC: Routine Practices and Additional Precautions, Table 5 — varicella
  • Health Canada midazolam monograph: anterograde amnesia
  • Clinical study: lack of association between atelectasis and fever
  • Association of Anaesthetists cell-salvage guideline: obstetric use
